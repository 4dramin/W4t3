Summarizing Two Opinions
Task 1.1 Guide — 15 Marks

📌 What is Task 1.1?

You read a passage that presents two different points of view on a topic, then write 4–5 sentences in your own words. Your answer must:

  • Identify View 1 — what one group believes
  • Identify View 2 — what the opposing group believes
  • Use your own vocabulary — do NOT copy phrases from the text
  • Write at least 4–5 clear sentences (aim for 80–120 words)

⚠️ Minimum length: 170 words in the passage. Your summary must be 4–5 sentences paraphrasing BOTH opinions.

🔢 4-Step Formula for Summarizing Two Opinions

  1. Step 1 — Introduce the debate: Start with a sentence that sets up the topic and the fact there are two views
  2. Step 2 — Present View 1: Use reporting language to paraphrase the first opinion (1–2 sentences)
  3. Step 3 — Present View 2: Use a contrast word then paraphrase the opposing view (1–2 sentences)
  4. Step 4 — Brief closing: Optionally add a final sentence summarizing the conflict

Passage theme: Modern skyscrapers vs. traditional Emirati architecture

Intro sentence The passage explores a debate about how urban design should develop in the UAE.

View 1 Supporters of modern construction argue that glass skyscrapers demonstrate the nation's impressive wealth and attract international visitors and business.

✅ Annotated Example — Nature or Nurture Theme

Intro The text presents two contrasting scientific views about what shapes human personality and behaviour.

View 1 Those who support the "nature" argument claim that genetics and biology determine a person's intelligence and character from birth, as shown by studies of identical twins raised apart.

View 2 On the other hand, "nurture" theorists insist that the environment — including parents, teachers and friends — has a far greater influence on how a person develops throughout life.

Closing The passage thus sets up a clear conflict between genetic destiny and the power of environmental experience.

📝 Full Essay Example Paragraphs — Nature vs Nurture

Introduction: Undeniably, scientists and philosophers have argued for centuries about what truly forms human personality and behaviour. While some experts believe that genetics determine our character from birth, others insist that the environment plays a far more powerful role. In my opinion, both nature and nurture contribute equally to making us who we are.
Body 1 — Inherited traits vs learned manners: First of all, family influences can shape a child in very different ways. Children inherit physical traits through genetics; however, they receive moral values through daily family education. Parents teach children how to behave correctly, which means that nurture directly shapes how genetic traits actually develop in practice.
Body 2 — Natural talents vs schooled skills: Furthermore, a student might have a natural talent for mathematics from birth due to their genetics. In contrast, they will never succeed if they do not practise those skills regularly at school. Therefore, hard work and dedicated teachers are essential to turn raw biological potential into real, measurable achievement.
Body 3 — Personal opinion: In addition, my personal viewpoint is that friends have a much bigger impact on character than genetics. While biology provides our physical bodies, our friends influence our daily choices and happiness. Choosing positive friendships encourages habits that no genetic code can supply.
Conclusion: In conclusion, human development is shaped by a complex mix of nature and nurture. Nevertheless, the combination of genetics and lived experience will always determine who we ultimately become as individuals.

Writing Mechanics
Spelling · Punctuation · Capitalization · Paragraphing

🔡 Capitalization Rules

  • First word of every sentence — always capitalize: The UAE…
  • Proper nouns — names of people, places, countries: Fatima, Dubai, the Emirates
  • Title words — capitalize main words in essay titles: UAE Architecture Past and Present
  • The pronoun "I" — always a capital letter: …what I believe…
  • Nationalities & languages — Emirati, Arabic, Japanese, English
  • Do NOT capitalize common nouns: the city, the building, the school (unless starting a sentence)
✅ Correct: "In my opinion, the Burj Khalifa in Dubai is a remarkable achievement."
❌ Wrong: "in my opinion, the burj khalifa in dubai is a Remarkable achievement."

🔤 Spelling Tips for Key Exam Words

🔑 Punctuation Rules

  • Full stop (.) — end every complete sentence. Never forget it!
  • Comma (,) — after introductory words (However, Furthermore, In contrast,) and to separate items in a list
  • Apostrophe (') — for possession (the UAE's architecture) and contractions (it's = it is). Note: its = belonging to it (no apostrophe)
  • Colon (:) — to introduce a list or explanation: There are two views: modern and traditional.
  • Semi-colon (;) — to join two closely related sentences: The old buildings used mud; the new ones use glass and steel.
  • Question mark (?) — only for direct questions. Academic essays rarely use them.
✅ "However, traditional buildings are perfectly suited to the desert climate."
❌ "However traditional buildings are perfectly suited to the desert climate." (missing comma after However)

📄 Paragraphing Rules

  • Every paragraph begins on a new line
  • Each paragraph has ONE main idea — introduced in the topic sentence
  • Supporting sentences explain and develop the topic sentence
  • An example or evidence proves your point
  • A linking sentence connects back to the main argument
  • Do NOT write one giant paragraph — break your essay into 5 clearly separate sections
Paragraph skeleton:
[Topic sentence — state the point]
[Explanation — develop the idea]
[Example — "For example, in the UAE…" / "I have heard that…"]
[Link — "Therefore, this shows that…"]

✍️ Sentence Structure — Avoid Common Mistakes

Error TypeWrong ❌Correct ✅
Run-on sentenceModern buildings use glass they look beautiful they attract tourists.Modern buildings use glass. They look beautiful and attract many tourists.
FragmentBecause the climate is hot.The buildings use cooling systems because the climate is hot.
Subject-verb agreementThe buildings was built in 1990.The buildings were built in 1990.
Wrong tenseIn the past, people uses wind towers.In the past, people used wind towers.
Missing articleThe architecture is sign of progress.The architecture is a sign of progress.

Transitional Words & Phrases
Connectives by Function — Click any word to copy

ℹ️ Why Transitional Words Matter

Transitional words and phrases are the "glue" of academic writing. They guide the reader from one idea to the next, show the relationship between ideas, and improve cohesion — a key marking criterion in both Task 1.1 and Task 1.2. Click any word below to copy it to your clipboard.

💡 Transitional Words in Context — Exam Themes

Addition: "Modern buildings are energy-efficient. Furthermore, they provide thousands of jobs for engineers and workers."
Contrast: "Traditional buildings used mud and coral stone. In contrast, modern skyscrapers are built entirely from imported steel and glass."
Comparison: "Makeup artists spend hours perfecting their skills. Similarly, fine art painters dedicate years to mastering their technique."
Result: "People were raised in different cultural environments. Consequently, their idea of what counts as beautiful differs significantly."
Example: "Some cultures have unique beauty customs. For instance, historical Japanese women blackened their teeth as a sign of beauty."

Templates & Sentence Starters
Fill-in frames for comparison & opinion essays

💡 Click any template to copy it. Replace the italic blanks with your own words. These frames help you start — but always personalise them!

📖 Introduction Templates

🏁 Template 1 — General Comparison
There is an ongoing debate about [topic]. While some people believe that [view A], others strongly argue that [view B]. In my opinion, [your thesis].
🏁 Template 2 — Historical vs Modern
The [topic] has changed dramatically over the last few decades. Today, both [aspect A] and [aspect B] reflect the incredible diversity of [field/culture].
🏁 Template 3 — Cultural Debate
There is a major global debate about whether [topic] is universal or entirely dependent on [culture/environment/genetics]. This essay will compare [point A] with [point B] before sharing my personal viewpoint.

📘 Body Paragraph 1 Templates — First Comparison Point

📌 Template 4 — PEEL (First Body)
First of all, [topic of this paragraph] shows a clear difference. In the past, people used neither [old item A] nor [old item B], so they relied only on [traditional method]. On the other hand, modern [people/societies] use not only [new item A] but also [new item B]. This clearly shows that [link to argument].
📌 Template 5 — Simple Contrast
To begin with, [aspect] differs significantly between the two sides. [Group A] believe that [view], which means [explanation]. In contrast, [Group B] prefer [alternative] because [reason].

📗 Body Paragraph 2 Templates — Second Comparison Point

📌 Template 6 — Showing Time Difference
Furthermore, [second comparison topic] shows another major difference, although [what both sides share] is always important. Long ago, people [past action/habit]. In contrast, modern [people/societies] [current action], which allows them to [benefit] much more easily.
📌 Template 7 — Adding Evidence
In addition to this, [second point]. For example, [specific example from text/life]. I have also heard that [something you know/have been told]. Therefore, this confirms that [link to your argument].

📙 Body Paragraph 3 Templates — Personal Opinion

📌 Template 8 — Opinion with Counter-argument
In addition, my personal viewpoint is that [your opinion]. While I truly respect [the other side], I firmly believe that [your view] is much better because [reason 1]. Moreover, [reason 2 or supporting detail], which proves that [final link].
📌 Template 9 — Opinion with Concession
Furthermore, my personal viewpoint is that [your position]. Admittedly, some people argue that [counter-view]. However, I believe [your rebuttal] because [reason]. Consequently, [link to argument].

📕 Conclusion Templates

🏁 Template 10 — Standard Conclusion
In conclusion, [restate topic in new words] has evolved significantly. Nevertheless, the [shared quality/goal] still connects [side A] with [side B].
🏁 Template 11 — Reflective Conclusion
To summarise, [topic] has shown both [aspect A] and [aspect B]. Ultimately, I believe that [final reflection], because [lasting reason].
